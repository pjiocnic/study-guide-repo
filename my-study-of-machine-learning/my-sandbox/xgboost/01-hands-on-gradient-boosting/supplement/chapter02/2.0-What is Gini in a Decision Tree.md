### 🧠 What is **Gini** in a Decision Tree?

**Gini impurity** is a **metric** used by decision trees to decide **which feature to split on** at each step.

---

### 📜 Origin of the Name:
- **"Gini"** comes from **Corrado Gini**, an Italian statistician and sociologist.
- He introduced the **Gini coefficient** in 1912 to measure **inequality** in income distribution.
- In machine learning, **Gini impurity** is inspired by that same idea — measuring **how mixed** or **unequal** the classes are in a node.

---

### 📌 Formal Definition:

> **Gini Impurity** measures the **probability** of incorrectly classifying a randomly chosen sample if it was randomly labeled according to the distribution of labels in that node.

---

### 📊 Formula:

For a node with classes \( c_1, c_2, ..., c_k \):

\[
\text{Gini} = 1 - \sum_{i=1}^k p_i^2
\]

Where:
- \( p_i \) is the proportion of class \( i \) in the node
- \( k \) is the number of classes

---

### 🧪 Example:

Let’s say in a node of 100 samples:
- 70 are class 0
- 30 are class 1

\[
p_0 = 0.7, \quad p_1 = 0.3
\]
\[
\text{Gini} = 1 - (0.7^2 + 0.3^2) = 1 - (0.49 + 0.09) = 1 - 0.58 = 0.42
\]

- **Lower Gini** means **purer** node (closer to all one class)
- Gini = 0: Perfect purity (all samples of one class)
- Gini = 0.5: Maximum impurity for binary classification (50/50 mix)

---

### 🔍 Why Use Gini?

- Fast to compute
- Works well in practice
- Default in **scikit-learn's** `DecisionTreeClassifier`

---

### 🆚 Gini vs Entropy (another impurity measure):

| Metric      | Formula                              | Range       | Bias              |
|-------------|--------------------------------------|-------------|-------------------|
| Gini        | \( 1 - \sum p_i^2 \)                 | 0 to ~0.5   | Biased towards large groups |
| Entropy     | \( -\sum p_i \log_2(p_i) \)          | 0 to 1      | More sensitive to changes in small groups |
