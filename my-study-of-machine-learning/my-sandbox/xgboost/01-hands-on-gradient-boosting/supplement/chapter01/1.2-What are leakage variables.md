## 🕵️‍♂️ What Are Leakage Variables?

> **Data leakage** happens when your model has access to **information during training that it wouldn’t have at prediction time**.

### 📌 Leakage Variables:
These are columns (features) that **leak future information**, making the model **unrealistically good during training**, but **useless or misleading in production**.

---

## 🎯 Why Leakage Is Dangerous

- It **inflates accuracy** during training and validation.
- It **fails in real-world deployment**, where leaked information isn't available.

---

## 🧠 Examples of Leakage

| Feature         | Target (label)     | Why It’s Leakage                                      |
|-----------------|--------------------|--------------------------------------------------------|
| `casual`, `registered` | `cnt`              | Their **sum is the target** — trivially predictable   |
| `amount_paid`   | `loan_default`     | You wouldn't know the payment **before predicting** default |
| `diagnosis_code`| `disease_pred`     | Diagnosis comes **after** symptoms, not before        |
| `future_stock_price` | `stock_buy_signal` | You're using **future info** to make today's decision |

---

## 🔍 How to Identify Leakage Variables

### ✅ 1. **Check for Definition-based Relationships**
- Is any column a **transformation or sum** of the target?
- Ask: _"Could I know this column in the real world before the prediction is needed?"_

### ✅ 2. **Temporal Leakage**
- Does the column contain data that is **recorded after** the prediction would be made?

> E.g., predicting `next_day_sales` using `total_sales_this_week` — you wouldn’t know that yet.

### ✅ 3. **High Correlation with the Target**
- Use:
  ```python
  df.corr()['cnt'].sort_values(ascending=False)
  ```
- If a column has **very high correlation (e.g., 0.98+)**, inspect it — it might be a direct derivative.

### ✅ 4. **Unrealistically Good Cross-Validation Scores**
- If model performance is **too perfect to be true**, inspect feature importance and top-correlated variables.

---

## 🔨 How to Prevent Data Leakage

1. **Know your data's timeline** — don't use future info.
2. **Simulate the real-world scenario** — what inputs would be available at prediction time?
3. **Drop derived/target columns** (`cnt`, `casual`, `registered` case).
4. **Use pipelines** that strictly separate preprocessing between training and test data.

---

### ✅ Rule of Thumb:
> "If I were making a prediction in real time, **would this feature be available right now**?"

## ✅ **Data Leakage Detection Checklist**

Use this list to audit your features and prevent **leakage variables** from contaminating your model.

| ✔️ Step | What to Check | Why It Matters |
|--------|----------------|----------------|
| 🔍 **1. Target Derivation** | Is any feature a direct calculation or transformation of the target (e.g., `cnt = casual + registered`)? | The model will learn the answer instead of learning patterns. |
| ⏳ **2. Temporal Availability** | Would this feature be available **at prediction time** in real-world deployment? | Using future information violates real-time prediction assumptions. |
| 📅 **3. Timestamp Misuse** | Are you leaking future timestamps (e.g., total future sales, diagnosis date)? | Features recorded after prediction are invalid. |
| 📈 **4. High Target Correlation** | Does the feature have **very high correlation** with the target (`r > 0.95`)? | It may indicate direct leakage or post-outcome information. |
| 🧪 **5. Data Collection Process** | Are you clear on how and **when** each feature is collected? | Ambiguity in feature timing = potential leakage risk. |
| 📉 **6. Sudden Drop in Real Performance** | Does model perform **much worse in production** than in validation? | Hidden leakage may have inflated training scores. |
| 🔁 **7. Improper Preprocessing on Whole Dataset** | Did you normalize, impute, or encode data **before train-test split**? | Info from test data leaks into training. Always split first. |
| 📊 **8. Feature Importance Inspection** | Are strange features ranked high in feature importance? | Investigate top features — they may be leakage suspects. |
| 📦 **9. Leakage from Identifiers or Encoded Labels** | Are you using things like `user_id`, `zip_code`, or encoded categories directly? | High cardinality features may accidentally encode the target. |
| 🛠 **10. Too-Good-to-Be-True CV Score** | Is your RMSE/AUC/Accuracy unusually high? | Might be a sign of leakage or label memorization. |
