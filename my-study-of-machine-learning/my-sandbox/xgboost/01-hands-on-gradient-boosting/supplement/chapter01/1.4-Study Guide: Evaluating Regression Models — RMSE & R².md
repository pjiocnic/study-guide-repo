# 🚴‍♂️📘 Study Guide: Evaluating Regression Models — RMSE & R²

---

## 🎯 Objective

You're building models to predict **daily bike rental counts** (`cnt`), a **regression problem** — where the goal is to predict a continuous value (any non-negative integer).

---

## 📊 Step 1: Understand the Target — `cnt`

Summary statistics from:
```python
df_bikes['cnt'].describe()
```

| Metric     | Value     |
|------------|-----------|
| Mean       | 4504      |
| Std Dev    | 1937      |
| Min / Max  | 22 → 8714 |

This tells us:
- The **average number of daily rentals** is ~4500.
- The values vary **a lot** (std dev ≈ 1937).
- So predictions will need to be flexible enough to handle both busy and slow days.

---

## 🧪 Step 2: Linear Regression — Baseline Model

You train a simple **Linear Regression** model and get:

```text
RMSE: 898.21
```

### 🧠 What Does This Mean?

> The model's predictions are, on average, **off by ~898 bikes per day**.

Now compare it to the average:
```text
898 / 4504 ≈ 20%
```

That means your model is **about 80% accurate** in scale — not bad for a first model, but there's room for improvement.

---

## ⚙️ Step 3: XGBoost Regression — Improved Model

Now you train a more advanced model — **XGBoost**, which is great at capturing **nonlinear patterns**, interactions, and subtle effects.

It gives:

```text
RMSE: 693.21
```

### ✅ Why Is This Better?

- The average error drops by over **200 bikes/day**
- Relative error:
  ```text
  693 / 4504 ≈ 15.4%
  ```
- This means XGBoost explains the data **better** — especially in cases where the relationship between inputs and bike count is not a straight line.

> So, XGBoost is **more accurate and robust** than Linear Regression on this problem.

---

## 📐 Step 4: Bring in the R² Score

Now you introduce:

```python
from sklearn.metrics import r2_score
r2 = r2_score(y_test, y_pred)
```

Let’s say the results are:

| Model              | RMSE     | R² Score |
|--------------------|----------|----------|
| Linear Regression  | 898.21   | 0.71     |
| XGBoost Regressor  | 693.21   | 0.82     |

### 🔍 What is R²?

> R² (Coefficient of Determination) tells you:
>
> **“How much of the variation in bike rental counts your model is able to explain.”**

- R² = 1 → Perfect predictions
- R² = 0 → No better than guessing the average
- R² < 0 → Worse than guessing!

### 🧠 Interpret Your Results:
- Linear Regression explains **71%** of the variation in rentals.
- XGBoost explains **82%** — meaning it captures more **real patterns**, not just noise.

---

## ✅ Summary Table

| Metric         | Linear Regression | XGBoost        | Ideal Direction |
|----------------|-------------------|----------------|-----------------|
| **RMSE**       | 898.21            | 693.21         | Lower is better |
| **% of Mean**  | ~20%              | ~15.4%         | Lower is better |
| **R² Score**   | 0.71              | 0.82           | Higher is better |

---

## 📌 TL;DR

| Concept  | Intuition |
|----------|-----------|
| **RMSE** | How far off your predictions are, on average (in actual units like bike count) |
| **R²**   | How much of the target’s variability your model can explain |
| **XGBoost** | Performs better than linear regression on this task — lower RMSE, higher R² |
